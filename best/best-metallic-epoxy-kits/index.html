FAQ

Do I need special tools for metallic epoxy that a standard epoxy job doesn't need?

The core tools (notched squeegee, low-RPM mixing drill) are the same. Some installers add a heat gun or propane torch for advanced pigment-movement techniques, which is optional and best practiced on a test pour first.

Can I do a small metallic accent area instead of the whole garage?

Yes — metallic is popular as an accent border or a small feature area precisely because it's technique-intensive on a large scale. A smaller test area, or a deliberately smaller feature zone, is a reasonable way to get the look with lower risk.

Why do these listings not show exact coverage rates per gallon?

Metallic coverage varies more than standard epoxy because it's applied at variable thickness by design (to encourage pigment movement) — manufacturers generally publish size-by-square-footage kit options rather than a fixed sq-ft-per-gallon number.

Is metallic epoxy more expensive than a standard flake floor?

Generally yes, both in material cost and in the added risk of a technique-heavy first attempt. If budget or first-timer risk tolerance is a concern, a flake system is the more forgiving and typically cheaper choice — see our full garage floor epoxy kit rankings.
