Why Photos on Your Google Business Profile Drive Calls

A Google Business Profile with no photos reads as unclaimed or out of business. Homeowners scroll past it before they ever see your name.

Your audit checks whether your Google Business Profile has real photos on file — not stock images, an empty gray placeholder, or a single logo. This is one of the cheapest fixes on the whole list, and it's also one of the highest-leverage: it changes what a homeowner sees before they ever reach your website.

This is the first impression, before the click

A homeowner searching "epoxy floor near me" sees your map-pack listing before your website. A gray silhouette where a photo should be reads as neglected or possibly out of business — the same way an empty storefront window would. Real photos are the difference between "someone runs this" and "did this place close?"

Volume matters more than you'd expect

This isn't a one-and-done task. Profiles with 10+ photos see roughly double the engagement of those with just a couple, and the gap widens sharply from there — profiles with 100+ photos see multiples more calls and direction requests than the average listing. Adding a handful of new job photos after every completed project compounds this over time.

What to actually upload

Real, recent job photos — exterior of the building if you have a shop, your crew on-site, and finished floors — beat generic stock or a single old logo every time. This is the same real-photo requirement your audit checks separately for your website's project photos; it's worth keeping one running folder of job photos you upload to both places.

FAQ

How many photos does my Google Business Profile actually need?

There's no hard minimum, but engagement climbs steadily with volume — profiles with 10 or more photos roughly double the engagement of those with just a few, and the effect keeps growing well past that.

Do I need a professional photographer?

No — real phone photos of actual job sites and finished floors outperform generic stock images. Authenticity matters more than production value here.

How often should I add new photos?

After every completed job is a reasonable habit — it keeps the profile looking active, which is itself a signal Google and homeowners both notice.
