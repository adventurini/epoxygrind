Why Your Page Title Decides Whether Google Sends You Anyone

The title tag is the blue link text Google shows in search results — and it's the single biggest lever on whether someone clicks you over the contractor next to you.

Your audit checks whether your homepage has a well-formed title tag: the right length, and something a homeowner searching for your service would actually recognize. A missing or generic title ("Home" or your business name with nothing else) doesn't just look unfinished — it's a real, measurable click-through-rate problem, and for local trades, often a "you don't show up for the search at all" problem.

What actually belongs in it

For a local contractor, the title tag should say what you do and where — something like "Epoxy Garage Floor Coatings in Fort Collins, CO | Mile High Coatings." That's it. Not a slogan, not just your business name (Google and homeowners both need to know what you do before they'll click), and not stuffed with every service and city you've ever worked in.

Why "just your business name" fails homeowners who don't know you yet

A returning customer will click your listing no matter what the title says — they already know who you are. A homeowner who's never heard of you, searching "epoxy floor cost [city]," is scanning ten blue links deciding which ones sound relevant. A title tag that's just your company name gives them nothing to go on, so it gets skipped in favor of a competitor whose title actually answers the question they typed.

Google will rewrite a bad one — for you, not in your favor

When your title is missing, too generic, or too long, Google frequently generates its own version for the search snippet, pulled from whatever text it decides is relevant on the page. That's out of your control, and it's not written to convert — it's written to match a query. Writing your own accurate, specific title tag is the only way to control what a homeowner sees before they click.

FAQ

How long should a title tag be?

Roughly 50-60 characters. Longer than that and Google truncates or rewrites it; shorter and you're leaving relevance signal on the table.

Should my business name be in the title tag?

Yes, but not alone and usually at the end — lead with what you do and where, e.g. "Epoxy Garage Floors in [City] | [Business Name]."

Does Google actually rewrite title tags?

Yes — frequently, when it judges the existing one too long, vague, or unhelpful for the query. Writing a clear, accurate one yourself is the best way to prevent that.
