Homeowners can tell — and it works against you

A stock photo of a garish, over-lit epoxy floor gets reused across hundreds of contractor websites, and homeowners have seen it before. It doesn't prove you did anything — if anything, it raises the question of why a real business would need to borrow someone else's photo instead of showing their own work.

The fix is a phone, not a photographer

Real photos don't need to be professionally shot. A well-lit phone photo of an actual finished floor, taken right after the job, reads as far more trustworthy than a polished but obviously generic stock image — because homeowners aren't grading photography, they're checking for proof this is a real, active business.

This compounds with your before/after and Google photos

The same photos you take for your website should also go on your Google Business Profile, and every job is a chance to grab a before/after pair — the single highest-converting image type in this trade. One habit (photograph every job, before and after) covers three separate parts of your audit at once.

FAQ

How many real project photos do I need?

The audit looks for at least 3 clearly real, relevant photos — below that, the whole image-quality score is capped regardless of anything else on the page.

Are phone photos good enough, or do I need a professional?

Phone photos are fine, and often perform better because they read as more authentic. Good lighting and a clean, uncluttered shot matter more than equipment.

Can I mix in a few stock photos for variety?

Better not to — even one or two obvious stock images undercut the credibility of the real photos next to them. Keep the gallery 100% real work.
