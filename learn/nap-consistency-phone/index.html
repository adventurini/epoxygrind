Why a Mismatched Phone Number Quietly Costs You Rankings and Trust

Two different phone numbers for the same business tells Google (and homeowners) that something's out of date — and one of them is probably wrong.

Your audit checks whether the phone number on your website matches the one listed on your Google Business Profile — a check called NAP consistency (Name, Address, Phone). It sounds like a small technical detail, but it's a real, measurable local-search ranking factor, and a real source of lost calls when a homeowner dials the wrong number.

How this actually happens

It's rarely intentional — a new tracking number gets added to the website for ad campaigns, an old number never gets updated after a move, or a web developer pulled the number from an outdated business card. Whatever the cause, Google now sees two different phone numbers claiming to be the same business, and can't be fully sure which one is current.

The ranking cost is real, not theoretical

Citation consistency (your NAP matching across your website, Google listing, and other directories) is a measurable factor in local ranking algorithms. A mismatch doesn't just confuse homeowners — it's a small but real drag on whether you show up in the map pack at all for searches in your area.

The homeowner cost is worse than the ranking cost

A homeowner who calls the number on your website and reaches a disconnected line, a different business, or an old answering service doesn't try again — they call the next contractor in the map pack instead. This is a pure, avoidable leak: the lead reached your site, was ready to call, and still didn't reach you.

FAQ

What exactly should match?

The phone number on your website's contact info and header/footer should be the identical number listed on your Google Business Profile — not a different tracking or forwarding number.

Can I use a call-tracking number for ads?

Yes, but keep your primary/organic listing number consistent everywhere else, and set up call forwarding rather than displaying two permanently different numbers.

How do I check this myself?

Search your business name on Google, note the number shown in the knowledge panel, then compare it against what's printed on your homepage and contact page.
