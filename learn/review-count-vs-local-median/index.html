Why Your Review Count Is Judged Against Local Competitors, Not a Fixed Number

A homeowner isn't asking "is 40 reviews good?" — they're asking "why does this contractor have 40 when the other three have 150?"

Your audit compares your Google review count against the median for other contractors serving your local market — not a fixed, one-size-fits-all number. That's deliberate: homeowners make this exact relative comparison in real time, scrolling through 3-4 map-pack listings side by side before they click on any of them.

It's a side-by-side comparison, not an absolute score

Every homeowner comparing epoxy contractors sees your listing next to your competitors' in the same map pack, in the same search. If the local median is 150 reviews and you have 40, that gap is visible immediately — even if 40 would look perfectly respectable in a market where everyone else has 25.

A stronger rating doesn't fully offset a lower count

A perfect 5.0 with a handful of reviews reads as new or untested next to a 4.7 with 150 — homeowners are handing over several thousand dollars for a job with no trial run, so a longer track record often wins the comparison even against a marginally lower star rating.

Closing the gap is a process, not a one-time push

A one-time push for reviews closes the gap briefly, then it reopens as competitors keep collecting theirs. The fix that actually holds is a standing habit — a text or email review request sent after every completed job, every time, so the count keeps climbing on its own instead of needing another push every few months.

FAQ

Is there a minimum number of reviews I need?

Roughly 20 is a meaningful floor — a large share of consumers won't consider a business below that. But the more important number is how you compare to the specific competitors homeowners see you next to.

How do I find out what my local median actually is?

Search the same terms a homeowner would (e.g. "epoxy garage floor [your city]") and note the review counts on the 3-5 listings above and around yours in the map pack.

What's the fastest way to close a review-count gap?

A consistent, automatic review request sent to every customer right after job completion — while the work is fresh — outperforms sporadic manual asks by a wide margin.
