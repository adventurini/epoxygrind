Why Reviews Need to Live on Your Website, Not Just Google

A homeowner who has to leave your site to go check your reviews on Google might not come back. Reviews shown right on the page close that gap.

Your audit checks whether real customer reviews appear directly on your website — not just linked out to your Google Business Profile. Your Google rating gets a homeowner to click through to your site in the first place; reviews shown on the page itself are what keeps them there and convinces them to actually submit the form.

Every extra click is a chance to lose them

Sending a homeowner away from your site to go read reviews on Google works against you the same way a buried lead form does — it's one more click, one more chance they get distracted, land on a competitor's listing instead, or simply don't bother. Reviews shown right on the page remove that detour entirely.

Placement next to the ask matters most

A review or two placed right next to your CTA or lead form does the most work — it answers "can I trust this business with a $3,000+ job" at the exact moment a homeowner is deciding whether to commit their contact information. Reviews buried in a separate testimonials page rarely get seen before that decision is already made.

Specific reviews outperform generic ones

"Great job!" does very little. A review that names the actual job — "redid our garage floor in a day, no cracking six months later" — reads as real and specific, which is exactly what makes a testimonial persuasive instead of forgettable.

FAQ

How many reviews should I show on my site?

A handful of specific, real reviews placed near your CTA does more work than a long wall of generic ones — quality and placement matter more than volume here.

Can I just embed my Google reviews widget?

Yes, that satisfies this check — the point is that reviews are visible on the page itself, not that they were manually retyped.

Where should reviews go on the page?

As close to your primary CTA or lead form as you can reasonably fit them — that's the exact moment a homeowner is deciding whether to trust you with their contact info.
