What Does a Professional Epoxy Job Actually Include?

Short answer: you're paying for equipment most people don't own, a moisture-mitigation judgment call, a higher-solids product tier, and a written warranty — not just labor to do what a DIY kit already includes.

A professional quote can look expensive next to a DIY kit price until you break down what's actually bundled in. Here's what a legitimate quote should include — and what to ask about if it's missing.

DIY if…

  • You're comfortable providing all of this yourself: equipment, moisture judgment, product research
  • The value of a warranty matters less to you than the upfront savings

Hire a pro if…

  • You want someone else to own the equipment, the judgment calls, and the warranty
  • The full bundle below is worth more to you than doing each piece yourself

Commercial-grade equipment and crew time

Diamond grinders, dust extraction, and application tools sized for production work — not the single rented unit a DIYer uses once. This is the most visible cost difference, but often not the most important one.

Moisture testing and mitigation

A professional installer doesn't just test slab moisture — they know what to DO when a test comes back marginal: a mitigation primer, extended dry time, or a different product tier entirely. This judgment call is where a lot of DIY jobs quietly go wrong, since the free test tells you there's a problem but not always the right fix.

A higher-solids product tier

Professional installs commonly use 100%-solids epoxy or polyaspartic rather than retail water-based formulas — see what solids content actually means for why this drives the durability difference covered in our durability comparison.

Licensing, insurance, and a written warranty

A legitimate contractor carries liability insurance (protecting you if something goes wrong during the job) and backs the install with a written warranty — typically covering adhesion and workmanship for years, not just "we did our best." FAQ

Why is a professional epoxy quote so much more than a DIY kit?

It bundles commercial equipment, moisture-mitigation judgment, a higher-solids product tier, and a written warranty — not just labor for the same steps a DIY kit covers.

Does a professional installer use a different product than DIY kits?

Often yes — typically 100%-solids epoxy or polyaspartic, which cost more per gallon but last significantly longer than retail water-based formulas.

What warranty should I expect from a professional epoxy install?

A written warranty covering adhesion and workmanship, with terms varying by installer — always get it in writing with specific coverage and duration before signing.

Is a professional epoxy installer required to be insured?

Licensing/insurance requirements vary by state, but any legitimate contractor should carry liability insurance and be willing to show proof before work starts.
